Pronunciation

How to pronounce vaporetto

Four syllables, stress on the third, and a double t you can actually hear. English speakers usually get the vowels right and the beat wrong — which is the part Italians notice.

Keep the e in RET short, as in “bed”, not “ray”. Hold the double t for a beat — the tongue stops the air, then releases. The final o is short and rounded, not an “oh” that trails off.

01 — The beats

Sound by sound

Italian is written almost as it sounds. Once you know the vowels, the word is mechanical.

Vaporetto, syllable by syllable
SyllableSoundEnglish approximationWatch out
va[va]“vah”Pure a, no “vay”.
po[po]“poh”Rounded o, short.
ret[ˈret]“ret” as in betThis is the stressed syllable.
to[to]“toh”Unstressed, but still a clear o.
The stress rule: vaporetto is stressed on the penultimate syllable, ret. That is the default for most Italian words ending in -o, and it is the single change that makes an English accent sound suddenly more Italian.

02 — English vs Italian

Both are acceptable. They are not identical.

English dictionaries give /ˌvæpəˈrɛtoʊ/: the first vowel reduces to a schwa, the r is an English r, and the final vowel is a diphthong. That is perfectly understandable in Venice and is what most English speakers say.

Italian keeps every vowel pure and taps the r once against the ridge behind the teeth. The double t is genuinely long — the closure lasts noticeably longer than a single t in English. You do not need to master this to be understood, but it is the difference between “vaporetto” and “vaporeto”.

If you only fix one thing, fix the stress. “Va-po-RET-to” is immediately recognisable; “va-PO-reh-to” makes people pause, even though every individual sound is close.

04 — Practice

Put it in a sentence

Pronunciation sticks faster in context. Try these three, keeping the stress on RET:

  • “We took the vaporetto to Murano.”
  • “The vaporetti are crowded at five o'clock.”
  • “Is this the right vaporetto for San Marco?”

Notice the plural: vaporetti ends in a clear “ee” sound — vah-poh-RET-tee. The stress does not move.

Words that share the root

Vapore (steam) is vah-POH-reh, with the stress on the second syllable. Vaporetto shifts it to the third. Evaporare (to evaporate) keeps the same Latin root but a different rhythm. Hearing the family helps: the vapor- part is always about steam, even when the boat is running on diesel.

05 — FAQ

Pronunciation questions

Is it “vaporetto” or “vaporetti”?

Both, depending on number. One boat is a vaporetto; two or more are vaporetti. The pronunciation changes only at the end: “-toh” becomes “-tee”.

Do Italians say “vaporetto” or “water bus”?

In Italian, vaporetto. In English-language signage and tourism material you will also see “water bus”. Venetians sometimes just say il vapore in casual speech.

Where does the stress go in vaporetti?

The same place: va-po-RET-tee. The plural ending does not pull the stress forward.
